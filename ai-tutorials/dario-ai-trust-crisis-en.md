# Dario Amodei on the AI Backlash: 'Fundamentally a Crisis of Trust'

> "I think it is fundamentally a crisis of trust. I think that ordinary people don't trust companies, governments, or the tech industry and always suspect that we are cooking up some new way to screw them over."

---

On Saturday, August 15, 2026, a man who mostly stays off social media wrote a long thread.

Anthropic cofounder and CEO Dario Amodei had been called out by name. Investor Gavin Baker argued — on the All-In podcast and on X — that Amodei's warnings about AI's dangers had helped fuel the American backlash against AI, especially the opposition to data centers. That he had "lost the argument" on regulation. And that as the incoming CEO of one of the world's most important companies, he owed his industry better PR.

Translation: **stop scaring people, say something nice.**

Amodei refused the frame. He didn't argue about whether AI is risky. He attacked the causal chain itself — the assumption that public hostility toward AI is something AI executives talked into existence. Here are the ten things worth pulling apart.

---

## 1. The Ask: Be Your Industry's Spokesperson

Baker's request was unusually explicit:

> "he is about to be the CEO of one of the most important companies in the world," Baker wrote, "I respectfully think he should make an effort to be a more positive advocate for his own industry."

Look at the premise smuggled into that sentence: brand management is part of the job description. And Baker isn't alone. Plenty of people argue that AI skepticism and government crackdowns are the natural consequence of executives issuing dire warnings — there's even a case that Anthropic's own safety warnings backfired into government action against its most powerful model.

This isn't a technical disagreement. It's a disagreement about **what a frontier lab CEO is for**: industry advocate, or risk discloser.

**My take:** The trouble with "say something nice" is that it assumes public sentiment is a messaging problem. If the public were that easy to manage, advertising agencies would run the world.

---

## 2. "My Messaging Isn't Actually Lopsided"

Amodei's first move was to reject the characterization:

> Amodei disagreed with the idea that his "messaging has been disproportionately negative." Instead, he said that his writing has been "about equally balanced between risks and benefits," and that he wrote his essay "Machines of Loving Grace" because he "didn't feel the AI industry was painting an inspiring enough picture of how the technology could radically transform the world for the better."
